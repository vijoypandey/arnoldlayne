01 / THE THESIS

The harness becomes something Arnold wears, not somewhere Arnold lives.

The metaphor is borrowed deliberately from Altered Carbon: consciousness moves between bodies — sleeves — without mistaking the body for the person.

Arnold is a smaller, practical version of the same architectural idea. Personality, instructions, memory, tools, conventions and accumulated context live outside any single model or harness, backed by markdown, code, and YAML on cloud storage. A new runtime reads that durable layer and reconstructs the system instead of starting from zero.

From one agent to a Universe

Persistent systems for the parts of life where taste and history compound.

Arnold proved the sleeve pattern. The same idea now powers domain systems whose durable state lives outside any one AI product — markdown, code, and YAML on cloud storage, commonly Google Drive — so they can be invoked from different capable harnesses without losing their identity.

06 / THE NAME

Born in 1967.
Still stealing clothes.

Arnold Layne was Pink Floyd's debut single, written by Syd Barrett and released in March 1967. The song's protagonist collected other people's clothes from washing lines. Mine collects context. Between Pink Floyd and Altered Carbon, the metaphors became unexpectedly appropriate: bodies change, sleeves change, brains change. Arnold remains.
